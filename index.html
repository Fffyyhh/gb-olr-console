GB-OLR

A gradient-boosting classifier produces a four-tier posterior from thirteen features. An ordinal logistic regression then re-calibrates it, together with the same thirteen features, under an explicit ordering constraint.

Layer 1. CatBoost, 500 trees, depth 6, learning rate 0.04. Postponing the tier decision gives the second layer a compact summary of the feature interactions.

Out of fold. Posteriors that enter layer 2 are always produced by a model that never saw the row being scored, so the second layer cannot learn in-sample optimism.

Layer 2. Sixteen standardised inputs: thirteen features plus P(Q1), P(Q3), P(Q4). One shared slope vector and three ordered cut-points; nineteen parameters in total, every one attached to a named input.

Reading the numbers

β > 0 pushes a paper toward Q1. The model uses the Pr(y ≥ j) parametrisation, the inverse of the usual Pr(y ≤ j) convention.

β·z is the local contribution of one paper. The sixteen terms sum to its ordinal index, so they are additive within a paper.

Expected position interpolates the four probabilities at tier mid-points (12.5 / 37.5 / 62.5 / 87.5). It is an interpolation, not a calibrated quantile.

Fold agreement counts how many fold models reach the same tier. Low values mean the paper sits on a boundary.

Caveat. The three posteriors are near-collinear with each other and with the raw features, so coefficient magnitudes are compressed and only signs and between-fold stability should be read.

Validation

Expanding window, five folds: train up to 2000 / 2003 / 2006 / 2009 / 2012, test the following three years. A paper published in 2013 is never scored by a model trained beyond 2012.
